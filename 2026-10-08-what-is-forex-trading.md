The foreign exchange (forex) market is where currencies are bought and sold against each other. It is decentralised, so there is no single exchange. Banks, brokers, companies and individual traders trade through a global network, and the market runs around the clock from Monday to Friday.

## Currency pairs

Currencies always trade in pairs. In EUR/USD, the first currency (EUR) is the **base** and the second (USD) is the **quote**. If EUR/USD is 1.0800, one euro costs 1.08 US dollars. When you buy the pair, you are betting the base will strengthen against the quote. When you sell, you are betting the opposite.

## Who trades forex?

Central banks, commercial banks, businesses paying for imports, investment funds and retail traders all take part. Most volume comes from institutions, which is why the biggest moves often follow interest rate decisions and economic data.

## Trading sessions

Activity follows the sun: Sydney, Tokyo, London and New York. The overlap of London and New York usually brings the most volume and the tightest spreads.

## Costs to know

Brokers earn from the **spread** (the gap between the buy and sell price) and sometimes commission. These costs matter more than beginners expect, especially on short-term trades.
