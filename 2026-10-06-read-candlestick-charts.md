Candlesticks pack four prices into one shape: the open, high, low and close for a chosen time period.

## Anatomy of a candle

The thick part is the **body**, which spans the open to the close. The thin lines are **wicks** (or shadows) and show the highest and lowest prices reached. In most charts, a bullish candle closes above its open and a bearish candle closes below it.

## What the shape suggests

A long body shows strong, one-sided pressure. Long wicks show that price pushed far in one direction and was rejected. A tiny body with wicks on both sides (a doji) shows indecision.

## Context matters

A single candle means little on its own. Look at where it forms: near a support or resistance level, after a long trend, or during a major news release. Confirm with the candles that follow before acting.

## Practice

Open a demo account, pick one pair and timeframe, and spend a week labelling candles without trading. You will start to see how buyers (bulls) and sellers (bears) take turns.
