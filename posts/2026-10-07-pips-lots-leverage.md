Three terms decide how much you win or lose on a forex trade: pips, lots and leverage.

## Pips

A pip is the standard unit of price movement. For most pairs it is the fourth decimal place, so a move in EUR/USD from 1.0800 to 1.0850 is 50 pips. For pairs with the Japanese yen, a pip is the second decimal place.

## Lots

A **standard lot** is 100,000 units of the base currency, a **mini lot** is 10,000 and a **micro lot** is 1,000. On a USD-quoted pair like EUR/USD, one pip is worth about $10 on a standard lot, $1 on a mini and $0.10 on a micro.

## Leverage

Leverage lets you control a large position with a small deposit, called margin. At 1:100, $1,000 of margin controls $100,000. That sounds powerful, but profit and loss are calculated on the full position size.

> A 50-pip loss on one standard lot is roughly $500. On a $1,000 account, that is half your balance in one trade.

## What to do with this

Decide how much of your account you will risk per trade (many educators suggest 1% or less), then choose a position size that fits that limit. Use a stop-loss, and start with micro lots while you learn.
